Nine stories

What I expected, what happened, what changed.

01

The wait is a screen, not a flicker

ExpectedA 1.2 s budget, covered by skeleton cards.
The engine showedAnswers take 5–40 s. Over 99.9% of that is the AI writing; the database takes milliseconds. The model reads 518 tokens/s but writes 8 (E1, E30).
Design changeA waiting card with the real steps, a timer and an honest range. Writing only the boxes the AI fills cut writing time by 47%.
02

A confident “No” is the most expensive failure

ExpectedThe main risk is a wrong number.
The engine showedThe worst failure was a wrong absence: invented product names made all six of Elena’s questions come back “nothing matches” (E18, E19).
Design changeA grounding check turns invented values into a hand‑off, and “nothing matches” always offers a way out (E22).
03

Make the AI’s job smaller before making it bigger

ExpectedA bigger model would be needed.
The engine showedSame model, same questions. Writing whole SQL scored 0/6; filling a form of allowed values scored 5/6. AI + rules scored 6/6 and 7/7 (E24, E26).
Design changeThe form became the contract’s constraints field, and the screen shows it as chips.
04

Overfitting, twice

ExpectedGood scores on the questions I tuned for.
The engine showedRules scored 6/6 on known questions and 5/7 on fresh ones. The router scored 35/35, then 4/8 fresh, sending two upset customers to a product list (E21, E32).
Design changeRules first, a one‑word AI tie‑breaker for the leftovers: 8/8 fresh (E33, E36).
05

Memory without a memory

ExpectedFollow‑ups need server‑side sessions.
The engine showed“Same chairs, but within 2 days” works by sending the previous form back. A speed fix once silently broke it; the regression run caught it (E23, E38, E40).
Design changeRequirements shown as removable chips, so a lost requirement is visible at a glance (R4).
06

Right for the wrong reason

ExpectedA correct total means a correct answer.
The engine showedElena asked for navy. The AI left colour out, the engine picked black, and the total was still right. The receipt pointed at the wrong chair (E25).
Design changeNo silent picks: “any colour, same price” with every colour cited, or “Which colour?”.
07

Ambiguity needs a rule, not a feeling

ExpectedAsk when unsure.
The engine showed“Navy shell” matched a chair, a guest chair and a jacket within 3% of each other (E15).
Design changeAsk when the top matches come from different categories: “Did you mean…?” chips with category and price (E34).
08

The contract grew because of testing

ExpectedThe contract is finished.
The engine showedtable was added to mark answers, trace to debug empty ones (E4).
Design change“Can we debug it?” and “can we mark it?” are design requirements too.
09

A sentence is a dead end for a screen

ExpectedThree review fixes are screen work.
The engine showedThe screen couldn’t make a hint tappable or say what “available: 48” was about, because the engine only sent sentences (E46).
Design changeThe contract now sends relax, drop and subject as data. Removals answer in 1–4 ms with no AI.
