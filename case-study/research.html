Case study/Appendix/A1 — Research & Discovery

Research & Discovery.

The users, pain points and constraints below are design‑time hypotheses, not findings from interviews I ran. Where the engine later tested a hypothesis, its result sits beside it.

Pain points

Four hypotheses, and what the engine showed.

Pain point AssumedWhat the build showed Measured
Confident wrong answers on price and stock. Text‑only RAG doesn’t query live tables.Real, and worse than expected. When an AI wrote whole SQL queries for multi‑rule questions, all 6 came back as a confident “No, nothing matches” while 48 chairs sat in a warehouse (E19).
Vocabulary mismatch in search. Shoppers and catalogs use different words.Reproduced. Keyword search missed “something to charge my laptop” (the product says charging cable). Meaning search found it (E9, E11).
Swivel‑chair work for support agents. Facts live in separate tools.Not tested. Marcus’s console is still a hand‑built prototype, not connected to the engine.
Black‑box trust barrier. Answers come without sources.Built into the contract: every card and lookup carries a receipt to its database row. Not yet tested with people.
Data freshness

Fast data is read live. Slow data can be indexed.

Fast · seconds

Stock and orders

Always read from the tables at question time, joined by SKU, so a search hit is never served with a stale stock number.

Medium · hours

Prices and delivery schedules

Also read at question time; delivery days come from a per‑warehouse, per‑city table.

Slow · months

Specs and policies

The only text that is pre‑indexed for keyword and meaning search.

Trust

A receipt proves where a number came from. Not that it’s right.

Three rules carried through from the first wireframes and are now enforced by the answer contract: every number has a receipt, the receipt opens in a drawer with the row and the query, and calculations show their working.

The build added a fourth rule nobody had planned. A rules‑only engine answered “What would 40 chairs cost?” with $320, the price of one chair, and attached a perfectly real citation. Correctness has to be checked separately, against an answer key.
