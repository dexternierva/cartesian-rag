UX & design engineering case study · Tabular RAG

CartesianRAG.

The engine got the answers right before the screens were ready for them.

I designed an assistant that answers a camp‑to‑desk store’s questions from its own tables, with a receipt for every number. Then I built the engine behind it, measured it, and let what I measured redesign the screens.

The design’s one big idea was a contract: every answer is a ticket with the rows it used, a receipt for each number, and the requirements it understood. Screens draw from the ticket, never from loose text. Marcus’s console was designed but is still a hand‑built prototype; only Elena’s half was built for real.

Decisions I’d defend


Decision 1
Let the AI fill a form, not write SQL
Considered: a bigger model writing whole queries.
0/6 → 5/6 with the same model. Code builds the SQL from checked values.
Decision 2
Rules first, AI only for the leftovers
Considered: AI on every question.
Known questions stay instant; the AI writes one word for the rest. Fresh routing 4/8 → 8/8.
Decision 3
Remove a requirement without asking the AI again
Considered: re‑sending a reworded question.
The form minus one box goes straight to the database: 1–4 ms, and it can’t mishear.
Decision 4
Replay real recordings on GitHub Pages
Considered: hand‑written demo answers, or a paid server.
Free and honest: every demo answer came out of the engine, and the status line says when.
Decision 5
Keep status colours out of the brand
Considered: brand green for “in stock”.
Lake blue + shape + word, so stock never depends on telling red from green.

What I learned about RAG




Six ideas, in plain words.


RAGRetrieval‑augmented generation
Look things up first, then answer from what you found.
An open‑book exam: the answer must come from the book on the desk.
RouterWhich desk handles this?
Rules recognise most questions; an AI picks one word for the rest.
A receptionist who knows most visitors by sight.
BM25 + embeddingsTwo kinds of search
Keyword search matches rare words; meaning search matches ideas.
Two detectives with different blind spots.
RRFReciprocal Rank Fusion
Each search gives a product 1 / (60 + its rank); the scores are added.
Two judges’ rankings combined into one.
Grounding checkIs that value real?
Before saying “no”, every value a query searched for is checked against the database.
A librarian who checks your spelling before saying “we don’t have it”.
ContractThe answer ticket
Every answer follows one JSON shape: rows, receipts, requirements, timing.
A receipt the screen knows how to read.
